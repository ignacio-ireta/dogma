# Business Vocabulary for Case Interviews

*Not MBA jargon — just the terms you need to sound fluent when talking about business problems. Organized by when you'd use them.*

---

## Talking About Money

| Term | What It Means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Revenue** | Total money coming in (before any costs) | "Revenue is up 5% year-over-year" |
| **Profit** | What's left after costs. Revenue - Costs. | "Profit dropped 20% despite higher revenue" |
| **Gross profit** | Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold (direct costs only) | "Gross profit margin is 40%" |
| **Net profit** | Revenue - ALL costs (including overhead, taxes) | "Net profit margin is 8%" |
| **Margin** | Profit as a percentage of revenue | "Their margin compressed from 15% to 10%" |
| **COGS** | Cost of Goods Sold — what it costs to make/buy the product | "COGS increased due to supplier price hikes" |
| **Operating expenses (OpEx)** | Day-to-day costs: rent, salaries, marketing | "OpEx grew faster than revenue" |
| **Capital expenditure (CapEx)** | Big one-time investments: equipment, buildings | "They invested $5M in new distribution centers" |
| **EBITDA** | Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, Amortization — a measure of operating performance | "EBITDA margin is a better comparison across companies" |
| **ROI** | Return on Investment: (gain - cost) / cost | "The campaign had a 3x ROI" |
| **Break-even** | The point where revenue = costs (no profit, no loss) | "Break-even at 10,000 units per month" |

## Talking About Customers

| Term | What It Means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Churn / attrition** | Customers leaving | "Monthly churn rate is 3.4%" |
| **Retention** | Keeping customers | "Retention rate improved to 92%" |
| **Acquisition** | Getting new customers | "Customer acquisition cost is $120" |
| **CAC** | Customer Acquisition Cost | "CAC has doubled in the last year" |
| **CLV / LTV** | Customer Lifetime Value — total revenue from one customer | "Average LTV is $2,400 over 3 years" |
| **Conversion rate** | % of visitors/leads who become customers | "Conversion improved from 2.3% to 3.5%" |
| **Segmentation** | Dividing customers into groups | "We segment by value tier and risk profile" |
| **Cohort** | A group of customers who share a characteristic (e.g., signed up same month) | "The Q1 cohort has higher retention than Q2" |
| **Penetration** | What % of the market you've reached | "Market penetration is 15% in urban areas" |

## Talking About Costs

| Term | What It Means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Fixed costs** | Don't change with sales volume: rent, salaries, insurance | "Fixed costs are $2M/month regardless of orders" |
| **Variable costs** | Scale with volume: materials, shipping, commissions | "Variable cost is $3.49 per unit" |
| **Unit economics** | Profit/loss per single unit or customer | "Unit economics are positive at $6.50 margin" |
| **Economies of scale** | Cost per unit decreases as volume increases | "At 1M units, production cost drops 20%" |
| **Overhead** | Indirect costs not tied to a specific product | "Overhead is eating into margins" |
| **Burn rate** | How fast a company spends money (especially startups) | "Monthly burn rate is $500K" |

## Talking About Growth & Strategy

| Term | What It Means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Year-over-year (YoY)** | Comparing same period across years | "Revenue grew 12% YoY" |
| **Quarter-over-quarter (QoQ)** | Comparing consecutive quarters | "Churn increased 2 points QoQ" |
| **Market share** | Your slice of the total market | "We hold 22% market share in SME" |
| **Addressable market (TAM)** | Total potential market size | "TAM for predictive maintenance in Europe is €1B" |
| **Competitive advantage / moat** | What makes you hard to beat | "Our data moat — 5 years of labeled outcomes" |
| **Scalable** | Can grow without proportional cost increase | "The model is scalable to all 15 hospitals" |
| **Pilot** | Small-scale test before full rollout | "We'd pilot with 3 stores before expanding" |
| **Rollout** | Full deployment | "Full rollout across all locations by Q3" |

## Talking About Models & Data (Business-Friendly)

| Instead of... | Say... | Why |
|--------------|--------|-----|
| "The model has 0.85 AUC" | "The model correctly identifies 85% of at-risk customers" | Non-technical stakeholders don't know AUC |
| "We'd use XGBoost" | "We'd use a gradient-boosted model — it's strong with this type of structured data" | Name the family, not the library |
| "Feature importance shows..." | "The key drivers of churn are..." | "Drivers" > "features" in business context |
| "False positive rate" | "The rate at which we flag someone who wouldn't actually churn" | Translate to the business meaning |
| "We'd optimize for recall" | "We'd tune the model to catch as many at-risk customers as possible, even if it means some false alarms" | Explain the tradeoff |
| "The data is imbalanced" | "Only 3% of customers actually churn, so we need to account for that rarity in our approach" | Context > jargon |
| "We'd deploy on SageMaker" | "We'd deploy the model in the cloud so it runs automatically on a schedule" | Platform names don't matter to clients |

## Phrases That Sound Natural in a Case

**Opening / Reframing:**
- "So if I understand correctly..."
- "The core question seems to be..."
- "Let me make sure I have the full picture..."

**Structuring:**
- "I'd break this into two main areas..."
- "Let me look at this from the cost side first..."
- "There are a few buckets to consider here..."

**Making assumptions:**
- "If we assume roughly X, that would mean..."
- "Let me put some numbers on this..."
- "A reasonable estimate would be..."

**Justifying choices:**
- "I'd go with X because..."
- "The tradeoff here is..."
- "I considered Y, but X fits better because..."

**Synthesizing:**
- "Pulling this together..."
- "The bottom line is..."
- "In terms of concrete next steps..."
- "The expected impact would be roughly..."
