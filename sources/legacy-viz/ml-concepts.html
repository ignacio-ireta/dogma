ML Concepts Visualized

Interactive explorations of core machine learning concepts — from bias-variance to ensemble methods.

Bias-Variance Tradeoff

See how model complexity affects the balance between underfitting and overfitting.

High bias (low complexity) = model is too simple and underfits. High variance (high complexity) = model memorizes noise and overfits. The sweet spot minimizes total error.

Overfitting: Training vs Validation Curves

Watch how training and validation loss evolve over epochs for different model complexities.

Underfitting: both curves are high. Good fit: both converge low with small gap. Overfitting: training loss keeps dropping but validation loss rises — the gap is the overfitting region.

L1 vs L2 Regularization

Visualize how Lasso (L1) and Ridge (L2) constrain model weights differently.

L1 (Lasso) pushes weights to exactly zero, producing sparse models (feature selection). L2 (Ridge) shrinks all weights toward zero evenly. Elastic Net combines both.

Gradient Descent

Watch an optimizer navigate a loss landscape. Adjust the learning rate to see convergence, oscillation, or divergence.

Too small: very slow convergence or gets stuck. Too large: oscillates wildly or diverges. Just right: smooth convergence to the minimum.

Bagging vs Boosting

Two strategies for building ensemble models: parallel averaging vs sequential error correction.

Bagging (e.g., Random Forest)
Boosting (e.g., XGBoost)
Bagging reduces variance by training models on random subsets in parallel, then averaging. Boosting reduces bias by training models sequentially, each correcting the mistakes of the previous.

Cross-Validation (K-Fold)

See how data is systematically split into training and test folds to estimate model performance.

Each fold takes a turn being the test set. The final score is the mean ± std across folds, giving a robust estimate of generalization performance.

Decision Boundaries

See how different classifiers partition the feature space to separate classes.

Logistic Regression creates a linear boundary. Simple and interpretable, but cannot capture nonlinear patterns.

K-Means Clustering

Step through the K-means algorithm: assign points to nearest centroid, then update centroids.

K-Means alternates between assignment (color points by nearest centroid) and update (move centroids to cluster means). It converges when assignments stop changing. The inertia (within-cluster sum of squares) measures compactness.

Feature Scaling

See how different scaling methods transform feature distributions.

Needed for: distance-based (KNN, SVM) and gradient-based (logistic regression, neural nets).
NOT needed for: tree-based models (Random Forest, XGBoost) — they split on feature values and are scale-invariant.
StandardScaler subtracts the mean and divides by std (z-score normalization). MinMaxScaler maps features to [0, 1]. Without scaling, features with larger ranges dominate distance calculations and gradient updates.
