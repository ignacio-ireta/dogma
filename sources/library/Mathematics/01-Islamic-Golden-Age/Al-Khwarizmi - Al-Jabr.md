# Al-Khwarizmi — Al-Jabr (~820 CE)

#person #milestone #seed

**Person:** Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (~780–850 CE)
**Published:** "Al-Kitab al-Mukhtasar fi Hisab al-Jabr wal-Muqabala" (~820 CE)
**Location:** Baghdad House of Wisdom
**Era:** [[Islamic Golden Age]]

## The Idea
Systematic methods for solving equations — written as prose, not symbols. The word "algebra" comes from "al-jabr" (restoration/completion). The word "algorithm" comes from his name.

## Context
- Scholars in Baghdad had just finished reading [[Archimedes]] and other Greek philosophers
- Hindu-Arabic numerals (0-9) spreading via Islamic world
- Equations still written as sentences: "a square and ten roots equal thirty-nine"
- Numbers thought of geometrically: a square IS a square, a cube IS a cube
- No negative numbers (can't have negative area!)
- No exponents beyond 3 (can't visualize 4D geometry)

## Why It Matters
- **Algebra was crucial to calculus** — you can't do derivatives without symbolic manipulation
- Bridged Greek geometry and modern symbolic math
- 800 years later, European mathematicians would build on this

## Leads To
- [[Viète - Symbolic Notation 1591]] — letters for unknowns
- [[Descartes - Analytic Geometry 1637]] — combining algebra with geometry
- The entire [[Algebraic Revolution]]
