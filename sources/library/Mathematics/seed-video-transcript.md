## Timeline of Ideas (from transcript)

### Ancient Greeks (~450–200 BCE)
- **Zeno** (~450 BCE): Paradoxes — dichotomy, Achilles, arrow
- **Eudoxus**: Method of exhaustion (attributed)
- **Archimedes** (~250 BCE): Circle area via pizza slices, proof by contradiction, "The Method"

### Islamic Golden Age (~800s)
- **Al-Khwarizmi** (~820): Al-Jabr — algebra as sentences
- Baghdad House of Wisdom scholars: volumes of solids of revolution

### Algebraic Revolution (1500s–1600s)
- **Viète** (1591): Letters for unknowns and constants
- **Descartes** (1637): Analytic geometry, coordinate system, standardized notation
- **Fermat** (~1630s): Tangent slopes via limit-like method, maxima/minima, integral power rule

### Indian Mathematics
- **Aryabhata** (500s): d/dx(sin) = cos — used for approximating sine values

### Early Integration (1600s)
- **Cavalieri** (1635): Indivisibles — cut shapes into infinitesimally small slices (controversial!)
- **Torricelli**: Gabriel's Horn — infinite surface area, finite volume

### Applied Math Bridge
- **Galileo** (~1600): Falling bodies on ramps, odd-number pattern → parabola
  - First hint: total distance (integration) related to rate (differentiation)
- **Kepler** (~1610): Equal area law in planetary orbits

### The Fundamental Theorem
- **Newton** (~1666): Fluxions, FTC, kept it hidden, Principia (1687)
- **Leibniz** (~1684): dy/dx notation, published first, popularized via social skills
- Both knew infinitesimals were "fictions of the mind"

### Post Newton-Leibniz
- **Euler** (1700s): Infinite series, moved calculus from geometry to functions, derived e
- Convergence tests era

### The Foundations Revolution (1800s)
- **Cauchy/Weierstrass**: Epsilon-delta limits — "the atom of calculus"
- Tamed infinity without using infinity
- **Set theory** (Cantor): Plays directly with infinity — still debated

## Key Themes
1. Integration came before differentiation (historically)
2. "Moral solution first, rigorous proof second" (Archimedes' method)
3. Ideas developed in pairs: algebra+geometry, integration+differentiation, math+physics
4. People in pairs: Galileo+Kepler, Descartes+Fermat, Newton+Leibniz
5. The whole story is about **jousting with infinity**
6. Teaching calculus historically > teaching it axiomatically (Bressoud's argument)
