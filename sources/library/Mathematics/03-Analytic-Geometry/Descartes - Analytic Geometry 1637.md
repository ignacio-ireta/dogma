# Descartes — Analytic Geometry (1637)

#person #milestone #seed

**Person:** René Descartes (1596–1650)
**Published:** "La Géométrie" (appendix to "Discours de la méthode," 1637)
**Era:** [[Scientific Revolution]]

## The Idea
Combine algebra with geometry: every curve is a family of equations, every equation is a curve. The **coordinate system** — x and y axes — lets you graph relationships between variables.

## Why This Was Revolutionary
- Ancient Greeks studied parabolas by cutting cones for centuries — without equations
- Before Descartes, comparing "money" to "cabbages" (two different quantities on one graph) was absurd
- Suddenly you could **analyze any curve** as an equation
- Standardized notation: x, y, z for variables; a, b, c for constants

## The Rivalry
- [[Fermat]] claimed he developed analytic geometry a decade earlier
- This "fierce lifelong rivalry" drove both to develop early derivative concepts
- Descartes found tangent slopes via circle-radius method
- Fermat found tangent slopes via the "h gets smaller" method (closer to modern derivatives)
