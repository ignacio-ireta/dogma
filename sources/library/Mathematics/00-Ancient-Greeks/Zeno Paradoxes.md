# Zeno's Paradoxes (~450 BCE)

#person #concept #milestone #seed

**Person:** Zeno of Elea (~490–430 BCE)
**Era:** [[Ancient Greece]]

## The Paradoxes
1. **Dichotomy:** To walk across a room, you must first go halfway, then half of that, then half again... infinite steps required → motion is impossible
2. **Achilles and the Tortoise:** Achilles can never catch a slower tortoise because he must first reach where it was
3. **Arrow:** A moving arrow is at rest at every instant → motion is an illusion

## Why It Matters
These aren't silly puzzles — they expose a fundamental problem: **how can you sum infinitely many things and get a finite result?** This question drove 2000+ years of mathematics.

## The Resolution (Eventually)
- [[Infinite Series]] — 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... = 1 (but *why*?)
- [[Epsilon-Delta Limits]] — the formal answer (1800s)
- Still debated: does the limit "reach" the value or just "approach" it?
