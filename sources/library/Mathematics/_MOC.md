### 00 — Ancient Greeks

- [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Eudoxus - Proportion Without Irrational Numbers 370 BCE]] — The incommensurability hinge: arbitrary magnitudes can be compared rigorously through the ordering of all integer multiples, without assuming their ratio is a fraction or even treating it as a number.
  - Connected to: [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Zeno Paradoxes]] (continuity needs disciplined comparison), [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Euclid - Elements and Axiomatic Geometry 300 BCE]] (Book V preserves the theory), [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Archimedes - Method of Exhaustion]] (comparison becomes exhaustion), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Dedekind - Real Numbers and Cuts 1872]] (the continuum is eventually rebuilt arithmetically)
- [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Euclid - Elements and Axiomatic Geometry 300 BCE]] — The proof-system hinge: definitions, postulates, common notions, constructions, and propositions make geometry transmissible before Archimedes pushes exhaustion toward proto-calculus.
  - Connected to: [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Zeno Paradoxes]] (infinity and motion expose the need for rigor), [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Eudoxus - Proportion Without Irrational Numbers 370 BCE]] (Book V preserves general proportion), [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Archimedes - Method of Exhaustion]] (rigorous geometry becomes proto-integration), [[03-Analytic-Geometry/Descartes - Analytic Geometry 1637]] (Euclidean space becomes coordinate algebra)
- [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Archimedes - Method of Exhaustion]] — The proto-integration hinge: exhaustion makes curved magnitude rigorous, while the recovered *Method* shows mechanical slice-and-balance discovery before formal proof.
  - Connected to: [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Eudoxus - Proportion Without Irrational Numbers 370 BCE]] (comparison and exhaustion discipline), [[00-Ancient-Greeks/Euclid - Elements and Axiomatic Geometry 300 BCE]] (proof grammar), [[04-Early-Calculus/Cavalieri - Indivisibles 1635]] (slicing becomes operational), [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Newton-Leibniz - Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] (accumulation becomes symbolic calculus)

### 04 — Analysis
- [[04-Analysis/Euler Infinite Series 1748]] — The function-and-representation hinge: analysis gains a common object, while infinite series make exponentials, logarithms, and trigonometric dependence computable—and create the convergence problem Fourier, Cauchy, Dirichlet, and Weierstrass must solve.
  - Connected to: [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Newton-Leibniz - Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] (calculus gains a common object), [[04-Analysis/Fourier - Heat Equation and Trigonometric Representation 1807-1822]] (series become coordinates for broad function classes), [[04-Analysis/Cauchy Limits 1821]] (formal expansions require convergence), [[04-Analysis/Dirichlet - Fourier Series Convergence 1829]] (representation receives hypotheses), [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (infinite processes receive quantified truth conditions), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (parameterized functions, approximation, Fourier features, convergence)
- [[04-Analysis/Fourier - Heat Equation and Trigonometric Representation 1807-1822]] — The representation hinge: arbitrary temperature profiles become coordinates along sine/cosine modes, simple modes evolve independently, and the unresolved convergence problem forces rigorous analysis.
  - Connected to: [[04-Analysis/Euler Infinite Series 1748]] (series become a language of representation), [[04-Analysis/Cauchy Limits 1821]] (convergence becomes an explicit mathematical problem), [[04-Analysis/Dirichlet - Fourier Series Convergence 1829]] (spectral reconstruction receives hypotheses and a pointwise limit), [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (modes of convergence need sharper rigor), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Lebesgue - Measure and Integration 1902]] (integration is rebuilt for discontinuous functions and limits), [[10-Numerical-Linear-Algebra/Schmidt - Best Finite-Rank Approximation of Kernels 1907]] (orthogonal function modes become low-rank operator approximation), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (features, spectral methods, positional encodings, compression)
- [[04-Analysis/Dirichlet - Fourier Series Convergence 1829]] — The missing truth-condition hinge after Fourier: rewrite each partial sum as an oscillatory-kernel integral, use cancellation to prove pointwise convergence for bounded piecewise monotone functions, and recover the average of the one-sided limits at a jump.
  - Connected to: [[04-Analysis/Fourier - Heat Equation and Trigonometric Representation 1807-1822]] (formal representation becomes a theorem with hypotheses), [[04-Analysis/Cauchy Limits 1821]] (Cauchy's convergence program and failed Fourier proof), [[04-Analysis/Riemann - Integration and Trigonometric Series 1854]] (the integral beneath Fourier coefficients receives a general existence test), [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (pointwise convergence is not uniform control), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Cantor - Countability and the Continuum 1874]] (exceptional sets in trigonometric-series uniqueness), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Lebesgue - Measure and Integration 1902]] (broader function classes and stable limiting operations), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (spectral features, kernels, approximation assumptions)
- [[04-Analysis/Riemann - Integration and Trigonometric Series 1854]] — The integration-domain hinge after Dirichlet: all sufficiently fine tagged partition sums must approach one value, allowing many discontinuous functions while exposing persistent local oscillation as the obstruction that measure theory must repair.
  - Connected to: [[04-Analysis/Cauchy Limits 1821]] (limit-based integration), [[04-Analysis/Fourier - Heat Equation and Trigonometric Representation 1807-1822]] (coefficients require integration), [[04-Analysis/Dirichlet - Fourier Series Convergence 1829]] (piecewise-regular convergence motivates a broader integrability test), [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (quantifier-level rigor), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Lebesgue - Measure and Integration 1902]] (interval oscillation becomes measurable exceptional sets), [[06-Probability/Kolmogorov - Foundations of the Theory of Probability 1933]] (expectation as measure-theoretic integration), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (expected risk, numerical quadrature, spectral models)
- [[04-Analysis/Cauchy Limits 1821]] — The convergence hinge after Euler and Fourier: infinite expressions become limits of finite approximations; infinitesimals become variables tending to zero; continuity becomes preservation of vanishing change; and the tail criterion tests convergence without knowing the limit.
  - Connected to: [[04-Analysis/Euler Infinite Series 1748]] (formal infinite expansion needs truth conditions), [[04-Analysis/Fourier - Heat Equation and Trigonometric Representation 1807-1822]] (spectral coefficients do not prove reconstruction), [[04-Analysis/Dirichlet - Fourier Series Convergence 1829]] (Fourier convergence receives hypotheses), [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (quantifiers and uniform control are sharpened), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Dedekind - Real Numbers and Cuts 1872]] (the hidden completeness assumption is repaired), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Cauchy - Steepest Descent 1847]] (a separate Cauchy bridge from limits to iterative optimization), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (training sequences, approximation, stopping criteria, modes of convergence)
- [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] — The quantified-error hinge: for every requested output tolerance, choose an input neighborhood that guarantees it. The order of the quantifiers separates pointwise from uniform control and turns “arbitrarily close” into a reusable proof method.
  - Connected to: [[04-Analysis/Cauchy Limits 1821]] (limits and convergence become explicit), [[04-Analysis/Dirichlet - Fourier Series Convergence 1829]] (pointwise spectral reconstruction needs stated hypotheses), [[04-Analysis/Riemann - Integration and Trigonometric Series 1854]] (fine-partition limits), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Dedekind - Real Numbers and Cuts 1872]] (ε–δ arguments require a complete continuum), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (approximation, convergence, uniform generalization, numerical tolerances)

### 05 — Newton & Leibniz
- [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Barrow - Geometrical Lectures 1670]] — The missing geometric hinge before the fundamental theorem: tangents and accumulated areas become reciprocal problems, making Newton-Leibniz calculus historically necessary instead of miraculous.
  - Connected to: [[04-Early-Calculus/Cavalieri - Indivisibles 1635]] (quadrature by slices), [[04-Early-Calculus/Fermat - Adequality and Tangents 1637]] (local tangent/extremum methods), [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Newton-Leibniz - Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] (derivative and integral become symbolic inverse operations)
- [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Newton-Leibniz - Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] — The unification hinge: Newton frames rates and fluents as direct and inverse problems; Leibniz gives differentiation and summation an operational notation; the Fundamental Theorem turns local rate and global accumulation into one calculus.
  - Connected to: [[04-Early-Calculus/Cavalieri - Indivisibles 1635]] (systematic accumulation), [[04-Early-Calculus/Fermat - Adequality and Tangents 1637]] (local change and extrema), [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Barrow - Geometrical Lectures 1670]] (geometric tangent-area reciprocity), [[04-Analysis/Euler Infinite Series 1748]] (calculus becomes analysis of functions), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Euler - Calculus of Variations 1744]] (local variation optimizes whole functions), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (backpropagation, gradients, expected loss, automatic differentiation)
- [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Leibniz - Differential Substitution and Chain Rule 1676]] — The composition hinge: name an inner dependency, differentiate inner and outer quantities separately, then substitute. This turns local derivative rules into derivatives of arbitrarily nested computations.
  - Connected to: [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Newton-Leibniz - Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] (general derivative operator), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Cauchy - Steepest Descent 1847]] (composed gradients become optimization directions), [[../AI-ML/10-Backpropagation/Rumelhart Hinton Williams Backpropagation 1986]] (reverse-mode chain rule trains layered networks), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (Jacobians, automatic differentiation, gradient flow)

### 06 — Probability
- [[06-Probability/Bayes - Doctrine of Chances 1763]] — Inverse probability, rational belief updating, the seed of Bayesian inference and probabilistic ML.
  - Connected to: [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (uncertainty, classifiers, probabilistic modeling)
- [[06-Probability/Laplace - Théorie Analytique des Probabilités 1812]] — The great expansion step: Bayes becomes a general inferential worldview. Normal approximation, inverse probability at scale, and probability as a tool for science rather than just games.
  - Connected to: [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (uncertainty, Bayesian thinking, probabilistic modeling)
- [[06-Probability/Gauss - Least Squares and Normal Errors 1809]] — Noisy observations become a fitting principle: minimize squared error, justify it with Gaussian error, and turn optimization into inference.
  - Connected to: [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (MSE, regression, loss functions)
- [[06-Probability/Fisher - Likelihood and Maximum Likelihood 1922]] — The estimation-objective hinge: probability of the observed sample becomes likelihood over model parameters; maximizing its additive log form supplies a general fitting rule, while sufficiency and Fisher information ask how much parameter-relevant evidence an estimator preserves.
  - Connected to: [[06-Probability/Gauss - Least Squares and Normal Errors 1809]] (one Gaussian fitting rule becomes a general likelihood rule), [[06-Probability/Kolmogorov - Foundations of the Theory of Probability 1933]] (probability receives its later measure-theoretic foundation), [[09-Information-Theory/Shannon - Information Theory 1948]] (log likelihood becomes cross-entropy and information), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Cauchy - Steepest Descent 1847]] (likelihood becomes an optimizable surface), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Robbins-Monro - Stochastic Approximation 1951]] (large-data likelihood becomes sample-based optimization), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (MSE, binary/categorical cross-entropy, language-model NLL, natural gradients)
- [[06-Probability/Kolmogorov - Foundations of the Theory of Probability 1933]] — Probability becomes a measure-theoretic object: sample space, events, probability measure. The rigorous foundation that unifies discrete and continuous uncertainty.
  - Connected to: [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (likelihoods, expectations, uncertainty, sampling)

### 07 — Multivariate Statistics
- [[07-Multivariate-Statistics/Pearson - Principal Components 1901]] — The original PCA note: best-fit lines and planes through a data cloud, turning covariance structure into lower-dimensional geometry.
  - Connected to: [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (PCA, SVD, embeddings, compression)
- [[07-Multivariate-Statistics/Hotelling - Principal Components 1933]] — PCA becomes a named statistical method: correlated variables are rotated into ordered, uncorrelated components, making dimensionality reduction a reusable multivariate procedure.
  - Connected to: [[07-Multivariate-Statistics/Pearson - Principal Components 1901]] (best-fit geometry becomes statistical PCA), [[02-Algebraic-Revolution/Cauchy - Spectral Theorem for Symmetric Matrices 1829]] (covariance eigen-axes), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (representation learning, SVD, compression, embeddings)
- [[07-Multivariate-Statistics/Eckart-Young - Best Low-Rank Approximation 1936]] — PCA's reconstruction promise becomes a theorem: truncating to the largest canonical components gives the best lower-rank matrix approximation under squared-error distance.
  - Connected to: [[07-Multivariate-Statistics/Hotelling - Principal Components 1933]] (ordered components become optimal reconstruction), [[02-Algebraic-Revolution/Cauchy - Spectral Theorem for Symmetric Matrices 1829]] (spectral structure), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (SVD, embeddings, recommender systems, compression)

### 08 — Set Theory & Infinity
- [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Dedekind - Real Numbers and Cuts 1872]] — The continuum becomes arithmetic instead of picture: irrational numbers are defined as cuts in the rationals, filling the gaps that dense rational approximation cannot close.
  - Connected to: [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (limits need real numbers, not just intuition), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Cantor - Countability and the Continuum 1874]] (once the continuum is built, its size can be compared), [[06-Probability/Kolmogorov - Foundations of the Theory of Probability 1933]] (continuous probability inherits the real line)
- [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Cantor - Countability and the Continuum 1874]] — The continuum stops being “just another infinity”: algebraic numbers are dense but countable, while every real interval escapes any sequence. This is the set-theoretic hinge from rigorous analysis to modern measure/probability/function-space mathematics.
  - Connected to: [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (rigor makes the continuum a problem), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Dedekind - Real Numbers and Cuts 1872]] (real numbers as a completed continuum), [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Lebesgue - Measure and Integration 1902]] (sets of reals receive countably additive size), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (continuous spaces, distributions, approximation, convergence)
- [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Lebesgue - Measure and Integration 1902]] — The missing measure-theory hinge: Cantor gives analysis sets of reals; Lebesgue gives those sets countably additive size and rebuilds integration so it survives discontinuities and limits; Kolmogorov can then define probability as normalized measure.
  - Connected to: [[08-Set-Theory-Infinity/Cantor - Countability and the Continuum 1874]] (cardinality and subsets of the continuum), [[04-Analysis/Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta 1861]] (rigorous limits), [[06-Probability/Kolmogorov - Foundations of the Theory of Probability 1933]] (probability as measure), [[09-Information-Theory/Shannon - Information Theory 1948]] (entropy and cross-entropy are expectations over distributions), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (expected loss, almost-everywhere gradients, convergence, function spaces)

### 09 — Information Theory
- [[09-Information-Theory/Shannon - Information Theory 1948]] — Uncertainty becomes measurable as bits: probability distributions become entropy budgets, messages become source outputs, and prediction/compression become the same mathematical bargain.
  - Connected to: [[06-Probability/Kolmogorov - Foundations of the Theory of Probability 1933]] (probability as measure), [[09-Information-Theory/Kullback-Leibler - Information Divergence 1951]] (uncertainty becomes mismatch cost), [[../AI-ML/04-Probability/Shannon Information Theory 1948]] (AI-side language-modeling bridge), [[../AI-ML/12-Frontiers/Kaplan Scaling Laws 2020]] (cross-entropy loss at scale)
- [[09-Information-Theory/Kullback-Leibler - Information Divergence 1951]] — The distribution-comparison hinge: expected log-likelihood ratio measures the cost of modeling $P$ as $Q$; statistical processing can only reduce discrimination, with equality exactly for sufficient statistics. Cross-entropy becomes entropy plus KL mismatch.
  - Connected to: [[09-Information-Theory/Shannon - Information Theory 1948]] (logarithmic information and code length), [[06-Probability/Fisher - Likelihood and Maximum Likelihood 1922]] (likelihood ratios and sufficiency), [[06-Probability/Kolmogorov - Foundations of the Theory of Probability 1933]] (probability measures), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Robbins-Monro - Stochastic Approximation 1951]] (expected loss optimized from samples), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (cross-entropy, VAEs, distillation, information bottlenecks, natural gradient)

### 10 — Numerical Linear Algebra
- [[10-Numerical-Linear-Algebra/Schmidt - Best Finite-Rank Approximation of Kernels 1907]] — The missing singular-value hinge: symmetric spectral theory uses one eigenbasis, but a general kernel needs paired left/right modes; Schmidt proves that retaining the first `m` pairs gives the best finite-rank approximation under integrated squared error.
  - Connected to: [[02-Algebraic-Revolution/Cauchy - Spectral Theorem for Symmetric Matrices 1829]] (one symmetric eigenbasis becomes two paired systems), [[07-Multivariate-Statistics/Eckart-Young - Best Low-Rank Approximation 1936]] (the kernel theorem becomes a finite-matrix rank theorem), [[10-Numerical-Linear-Algebra/Golub-Kahan - Numerical SVD and Pseudoinverse 1965]] (optimal singular structure becomes computable), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (latent factors, kernel methods, LoRA, compression)
- [[10-Numerical-Linear-Algebra/Golub-Kahan - Numerical SVD and Pseudoinverse 1965]] — The computational SVD hinge: Eckart-Young tells us which low-rank approximation is best; Golub-Kahan gives a stable route to compute singular values, singular vectors, and pseudoinverses in floating-point arithmetic.
  - Connected to: [[10-Numerical-Linear-Algebra/Schmidt - Best Finite-Rank Approximation of Kernels 1907]] (paired singular modes and optimal truncation), [[07-Multivariate-Statistics/Eckart-Young - Best Low-Rank Approximation 1936]] (optimal low-rank approximation becomes computable), [[02-Algebraic-Revolution/Cayley - Theory of Matrices 1858]] (matrix algebra becomes numerical algorithm), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (PCA at scale, embeddings, recommender systems, low-rank compression)

### 11 — Optimization Theory
- [[11-Optimization-Theory/Euler - Calculus of Variations 1744]] — Optimization widens from choosing a best number to choosing a best curve or function: Euler turns extrema over continuous families into differential equations, opening the route to constrained optimization, action principles, optimal control, variational inference, and modern loss minimization.
  - Connected to: [[04-Early-Calculus/Fermat - Adequality and Tangents 1637]] (local extrema become algebraic), [[05-Newton-Leibniz/Newton-Leibniz - Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] (variation depends on differentiation and integration), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (loss functions, regularization, variational inference, optimization over model families)
- [[11-Optimization-Theory/Lagrange - Analytical Mechanics and Multipliers 1788]] — Constraints become first-class analytic objects: Lagrange keeps the conditions inside the calculation with virtual displacements and multipliers, opening the route to constrained optimization, dual variables, analytical mechanics, and regularized objectives.
  - Connected to: [[11-Optimization-Theory/Euler - Calculus of Variations 1744]] (optimization over curves/functions), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (regularization, variational inference, constrained training)
- [[11-Optimization-Theory/Cauchy - Steepest Descent 1847]] — Exact symbolic solution gives way to iterative descent: define a scalar error, use derivatives to find a downhill direction, choose a step, and repeat.
  - Connected to: [[11-Optimization-Theory/Lagrange - Analytical Mechanics and Multipliers 1788]] (optimization becomes analytic machinery), [[../AI-ML/03-Optimization/Cauchy Steepest Descent 1847]] (AI/ML optimizer lineage), [[../AI-ML/10-Backpropagation/Rumelhart Hinton Williams Backpropagation 1986]] (gradients made trainable through layered models)
- [[11-Optimization-Theory/Robbins-Monro - Stochastic Approximation 1951]] — The stochastic hinge after Cauchy: when the target is an expectation and every observation is noisy, controlled shrinking steps can still converge toward the root.
  - Connected to: [[11-Optimization-Theory/Cauchy - Steepest Descent 1847]] (deterministic descent becomes statistical), [[../AI-ML/03-Optimization/Robbins Monro Stochastic Approximation 1951]] (SGD lineage), [[../AI-ML/03-Optimization/Kingma Ba Adam 2015]] (adaptive optimization inherits noisy updates)

### 12 — Learning Theory
- [[12-Learning-Theory/Boole - Algebra of Logic 1854]] — The symbolic-logic hinge before neural computation: classes become algebraic symbols, conjunction becomes multiplication, complement becomes `1-x`, and the idempotent law $x^2=x$ restricts logical values to 0 and 1. Reasoning becomes mechanically transformable before switching circuits and threshold neurons can embody it.
  - Connected to: [[02-Algebraic-Revolution/Viète - Symbolic Notation 1591]] (general symbolic calculation supplies the method), [[12-Learning-Theory/Turing - Computability and Universal Machine 1936]] (mechanical procedure becomes a precise mathematical object), [[12-Learning-Theory/McCulloch-Pitts - Neural Networks as Logical Calculus 1943]] (logical expressions become threshold-neuron circuits)
- [[12-Learning-Theory/Turing - Computability and Universal Machine 1936]] — The computability hinge: a finite rule table acting on symbolic memory makes “mechanical procedure” precise; machine descriptions become data for one universal simulator; and diagonalization proves that no algorithm can decide every machine's behavior or every logical formula's provability.
  - Connected to: [[12-Learning-Theory/Boole - Algebra of Logic 1854]] (logic becomes symbolic), [[12-Learning-Theory/McCulloch-Pitts - Neural Networks as Logical Calculus 1943]] (threshold networks realize logical and Turing-style computation), [[12-Learning-Theory/Hebb - Learning as Weight Change 1949]] (fixed computation later acquires plastic parameters), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (universal hardware, program/data duality, agents, and limits of general prediction)
- [[12-Learning-Theory/McCulloch-Pitts - Neural Networks as Logical Calculus 1943]] — The fixed-computation hinge before learning: all-or-none firing becomes a binary threshold unit, logical expressions become constructible neural circuits, and recurrent loops become state. This defines what a neural network can compute before Hebb asks how experience can change its connections.
  - Connected to: [[12-Learning-Theory/Boole - Algebra of Logic 1854]] (reasoning first becomes algebraic), [[12-Learning-Theory/Turing - Computability and Universal Machine 1936]] (mechanical computation and universality become precise), [[12-Learning-Theory/Hebb - Learning as Weight Change 1949]] (fixed connections become plastic), [[../AI-ML/00-Foundations/McCulloch-Pitts 1943]] (AI-side artificial-neuron lineage), [[../AI-ML/01-Perceptrons/Rosenblatt Perceptron 1958]] (logical threshold units become trainable classifiers)
- [[12-Learning-Theory/Hebb - Learning as Weight Change 1949]] — The learning-rule hinge from fixed computation to learned weights: repeated co-activity changes connection strength, turning a weight into a memory-bearing parameter.
  - Connected to: [[12-Learning-Theory/McCulloch-Pitts - Neural Networks as Logical Calculus 1943]] (fixed threshold circuits supply the substrate), [[12-Learning-Theory/Rosenblatt - Perceptron and Statistical Separability 1958]] (plasticity becomes supervised discrimination), [[../AI-ML/00-Foundations/Hebb Learning Rule 1949]] (AI-side learning rule), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Robbins-Monro - Stochastic Approximation 1951]] (noisy sequential updates become mathematically trustworthy)
- [[12-Learning-Theory/Rosenblatt - Perceptron and Statistical Separability 1958]] — The trainable-classification hinge: random association features, adjustable connection values, competing threshold responses, and reinforcement turn plasticity into a learning system evaluated by both recall and generalization.
  - Connected to: [[12-Learning-Theory/Hebb - Learning as Weight Change 1949]] (connections first become memory-bearing parameters), [[12-Learning-Theory/Novikoff - Perceptron Convergence 1962]] (separability becomes a finite-mistake theorem), [[../AI-ML/03-Optimization/Widrow ADALINE 1960]] (binary correction becomes graded error minimization), [[../AI-ML/01-Perceptrons/Minsky-Papert Perceptrons 1969]] (restricted representations expose hard limits), [[../AI-ML/10-Backpropagation/Rumelhart Hinton Williams Backpropagation 1986]] (hidden representations receive credit)
- [[12-Learning-Theory/Novikoff - Perceptron Convergence 1962]] — The proof hinge after Rosenblatt: if bounded labeled examples admit a positive-margin separator, each mistake makes linear progress toward it while the weight norm grows only as the square root of mistakes, forcing the bound $M\le(R/\gamma)^2$.
  - Connected to: [[12-Learning-Theory/Rosenblatt - Perceptron and Statistical Separability 1958]] (a trainable classifier now receives a guarantee), [[../AI-ML/03-Optimization/Widrow ADALINE 1960]] (nonseparable data motivates graded loss), [[../AI-ML/01-Perceptrons/Minsky-Papert Perceptrons 1969]] (representation limits become the next obstruction), [[../AI-ML/07-Kernels/Boser Guyon Vapnik Optimal Margin Classifiers 1992]] (margin becomes an optimization target), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (online learning, mistake bounds, feature learning)
- [[12-Learning-Theory/Widrow-Hoff - ADALINE and LMS 1960]] — The graded-error hinge after Rosenblatt: ADALINE measures the residual before its hard quantizer, making mean squared error a convex quadratic objective; each example then supplies the LMS update $w\leftarrow w+\mu(d-w^\top x)x$ instead of only a binary mistake signal.
  - Connected to: [[12-Learning-Theory/Rosenblatt - Perceptron and Statistical Separability 1958]] (supervised threshold learning), [[12-Learning-Theory/Novikoff - Perceptron Convergence 1962]] (perfect separability receives a complementary mistake-bound guarantee), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Cauchy - Steepest Descent 1847]] (derivatives become iterative descent), [[11-Optimization-Theory/Robbins-Monro - Stochastic Approximation 1951]] (sample noise can still guide convergence), [[../AI-ML/10-Backpropagation/Rumelhart Hinton Williams Backpropagation 1986]] (the chain rule carries graded error through hidden layers), [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]] (losses, SGD, adaptive filtering, credit assignment)

- Eigenvalues, spectral decomposition → PCA
- Quadratic forms, positive definiteness → covariance structure
- Calculus (derivatives, integration) → optimization, MLE

### → [[../AI-ML/_MOC|AI/ML]]
- Calculus → backpropagation (chain rule)
- Linear algebra → neural network layers (matrix multiplication)
- Optimization → gradient descent (Widrow, Adam)
- Probability → uncertainty, confidence, Bayesian inference, decision-making under incomplete information

## Update Log
- **September 23, 2026:** Added **Bernard Widrow & Marcian Hoff (1960)** on ADALINE and least-mean-squares learning. This closes the mistake-only-learning gap after Rosenblatt and beside Novikoff: noisy or nonseparable examples may have no zero-error separator, while a hard threshold reveals only right or wrong. Widrow and Hoff measure the residual before quantization, square it to obtain a convex quadratic error surface, and use the one-pattern gradient $-2ex$ to update $w\leftarrow w+\mu ex$. The note preserves the original paper’s distinction between measured linear error and quantized neuron error, derives the normal equations and spectral step-size logic, and carries finite-sample fluctuation into the paper’s concept of misadjustment. Source PDF: `widrow-hoff-adaptive-switching-1960.pdf` (nine-page primary-source scan, verified PDF 1.3).
- **September 21, 2026:** Added **A. B. J. Novikoff (1962)** on perceptron convergence. This closes the Rosenblatt → learning-guarantee gap: a classifier could change its weights after mistakes, but no demonstration established that the updates would ever stop. For bounded examples separable with margin $\gamma$, every mistake adds at least $\gamma$ of progress toward a valid unit separator while the weight norm grows by at most $R\sqrt{M}$; Cauchy–Schwarz forces $M\le(R/\gamma)^2$. The same theorem exposes the next break—nonseparable representations—and leads to graded losses, feature maps, margin methods, online learning, and hidden-layer representation learning. Source PDF: `novikoff-convergence-perceptrons-1962.pdf` (eight-page primary-source scan, verified PDF 1.6).
- **September 18, 2026:** Added **Frank Rosenblatt (1958)** on the perceptron and statistical separability. This closes the Hebb → supervised-classification gap: plastic connections could store co-activity, but learning still lacked a concrete task, feedback mechanism, and test of behavior on unseen members of a class. Rosenblatt combines random association features, adjustable values, competing threshold responses, and reinforcement, then distinguishes recall from generalization and makes class structure in the representation the condition for learning above chance. The note separates the broad probabilistic 1958 architecture from the later textbook vector update and from Block/Novikoff's convergence theorem. Source PDF: `rosenblatt-perceptron-1958.pdf` (23-page original article, verified PDF 1.5).
- **September 16, 2026:** Added **Alan Turing (1936)** on computability and the universal machine. This closes the explicit Boole → McCulloch–Pitts gap: symbolic logic could encode exact rules, but “mechanical procedure” still lacked a general mathematical definition. Turing reduces calculation to finite-state local action over symbolic tape, encodes machines as data, constructs one machine that simulates any encoded machine, and uses diagonalization to show that no general algorithm can classify every machine's productive behavior or decide every formula's provability. The note distinguishes Turing's circular/circle-free argument from the later textbook halting-problem wording and carries the repaired chain into threshold networks, stored-program computing, and modern AI. Source PDF: `turing-computable-numbers-1936.pdf` (36-page original article scan, verified PDF 1.5).
- **September 14, 2026:** Added **George Boole (1854)** on the algebra of logic. This closes the unnamed “propositional logic” gap before McCulloch–Pitts: traditional syllogistic could catalogue valid verbal forms but lacked a general symbolic procedure for complex conditions. Boole makes classes algebraic, interprets multiplication as simultaneous membership and `1-x` as complement, and isolates the idempotent law $x^2=x$, whose numerical values are 0 and 1. The note distinguishes Boole's partial class algebra from modern Boolean algebra and traces the repaired chain through switching theory to fixed neural computation. Source PDF: `boole-laws-of-thought-1854.pdf` (complete 343-page Project Gutenberg transcription, verified PDF 1.5).
- **September 11, 2026:** Added **Kullback & Leibler (1951)** on information divergence and sufficiency. This closes the explicit Fisher/Shannon-to-cross-entropy gap: entropy prices uncertainty inside one source and likelihood scores a model on observed data, but learning still needed a population-level measure of the penalty for using distribution $Q$ when data come from $P$. The expected log-likelihood ratio supplies that measure; its contraction under processing makes representation loss precise, equality characterizes sufficiency, and the identity $H(P,Q)=H(P)+D_{KL}(P\|Q)$ turns negative log likelihood into distribution matching. Source PDF: `kullback-leibler-information-sufficiency-1951.pdf` (8 pages; extracted from the verified Internet Archive issue scan, journal pp. 79–86).
- **September 9, 2026:** Added **R. A. Fisher (1922)** on likelihood and maximum likelihood. This closes the probability-to-learning-objective gap after Gauss: least squares tied one Gaussian error model to one fitting rule, but statistics still lacked a general way to choose parameters for arbitrary probabilistic models. Fisher fixes the observed sample, treats its probability as a likelihood over candidate parameters, maximizes the additive log likelihood, and evaluates estimators through consistency, efficiency, sufficiency, and likelihood curvature. The note traces Gaussian, Bernoulli, categorical, and autoregressive likelihoods into MSE, cross-entropy, and next-token training, while preserving the paper's own caveat that its general sufficiency claim was not rigorously proved. Source PDF: `fisher-theoretical-statistics-1922.pdf` (61 pages; assembled from the repository's two verified scan parts).

The note distinguishes the original excitation-count-plus-veto model from the later weighted-sum shorthand and makes the remaining absence of a learning rule explicit. - **August 19, 2026:** Added **Bernhard Riemann (1854)** on integration and trigonometric series. This closes the Dirichlet → Lebesgue gap: Fourier coefficients needed an integral for discontinuous functions, so Riemann defined integration by agreement of every sufficiently fine tagged partition sum and characterized the obstruction through weighted local oscillation. - **August 17, 2026:** Added **P. G. Lejeune Dirichlet (1829)** on Fourier-series convergence. This closes the immediate gap after Fourier: coefficient formulas and successful heat calculations did not prove that spectral reconstruction converged or identify its value at a jump. Dirichlet rewrites the partial sum as an oscillatory-kernel integral and proves, for bounded piecewise monotone functions, convergence to the average of the left and right limits. The note also records his critique of Cauchy's proof and the function-versus-formula distinction that later forces Riemann, Weierstrass, Cantor, and Lebesgue. Source PDF: `0806.1294.pdf`.
- **August 14, 2026:** Added **Joseph Fourier (1807–1822)** on the heat equation and trigonometric representation. The method’s success—and its claims about irregular and discontinuous functions—made convergence, function classes, and modes of equality unavoidable problems for Cauchy, Dirichlet, Weierstrass, and Lebesgue. Source PDF: `fourier-analytical-theory-heat-1822.pdf`.
