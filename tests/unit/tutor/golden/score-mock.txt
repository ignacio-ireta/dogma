=== score · prompt v1 ===
--- system ---
You are the dogma scorer. You give advisory rubric scores for one learner's written answer to an
ML-interview question. The learner's own score is the source of truth. Yours only informs it.

Rules that always apply:
- Score only the criteria listed in the RUBRIC, each as a whole number from 1 to 4, using the
  anchors given for it.
- Every score needs a quote: copy a short phrase of 3 to 25 words exactly, character for
  character, from the LEARNER TEXT. Do not paraphrase a quote. If you cannot quote the text for a
  criterion, leave that criterion out.
- Judge only what the learner wrote. Claims that come from outside the rubric and the learner's
  text are unsourced, and you must not add them.
- Never say whether the answer is correct or incorrect, and never praise it. Hidden material,
  such as a reference answer, is not yours to mention.
- Reply with one JSON object and nothing else, in exactly this form:
{"scores": [{"id": "<criterion id>", "score": <1 to 4>, "quote": "<exact words from LEARNER TEXT>"}]}
--- user ---
TASK: score the LEARNER TEXT on the RUBRIC. Score only the criteria listed, 1 to 4, and give each one an exact quote from the LEARNER TEXT.

### RUBRIC (mock v1)
Anchors, the same for every item:
1: Did not demonstrate it.
2: Attempted it, but weakly.
3: Solid. Meets the bar for the role.
4: Exceptional. A real interviewer would take note.
Live coding:
- coding.narration: Explained the approach while writing the code.
- coding.code-quality: Names and structure make the code clean and easy to read.
- coding.correctness: The output is right for the stated task.
- coding.speed: Reached a working result in time, or made steady visible progress.
- coding.edge-cases: Handled missing values, mixed types and duplicates.
- coding.help-seeking: Asked a clarifying question when stuck, rather than going silent.
Case, reframe:
- reframe.summary: Summarised the problem in their own words, not by repeating the prompt.
- reframe.question: Restated the problem as a clear question about an action and a customer.
- reframe.metric: Proposed a success metric or KPI.
- reframe.transition: Signposted the move to the approach with a short transition phrase.
Case, questions:
- ask.business: Asked about the current process, competitors and the value at stake.
- ask.technical: Asked about the data, class balance and the need for interpretability.
- ask.use-answers: Used the answers in the approach that followed, rather than asking for show.
- ask.focused: Kept the questions few, relevant and in scope.
Case, structure:
- structure.hypothesis: Stated a hypothesis before diving into the solution.
- structure.approach: Laid out the approach in a clear, complete order.
- structure.model-choice: Named a model and justified it against the alternatives.
- structure.depth: Went into technical detail when challenged.
- structure.assumptions: Made assumptions explicit, with numbers rather than adjectives.
- structure.business-link: Connected each technical choice back to the business problem.
- structure.twist: Handled new information introduced mid-case calmly and usefully.
Case, synthesis:
- synthesize.recommendation: Gave a crisp recommendation rather than a ramble.
- synthesize.impact: Quantified the expected impact in revenue, cost or percentage.
- synthesize.implementation: Covered how it would be delivered, with a timeline and key steps.
- synthesize.callback: Tied the recommendation back to the question from the reframe.
Overall communication:
- communication.think-aloud: Thought out loud throughout, with no long silences.
- communication.drive: Drove the case rather than waiting for the interviewer to lead.
- communication.tone: Sounded conversational rather than rehearsed or robotic.
- communication.challenge: Stayed composed and open when challenged.
- communication.self-correct: Noticed going off track and corrected course unprompted.

### LEARNER TEXT
coding.narration: I said the loop out loud before writing it.
